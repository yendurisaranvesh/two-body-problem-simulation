What am I looking at?

The idea

Two bodies pull on each other with gravity. The force is equal and opposite (Newton's third law), and its size is F = G m₁ m₂ / r², so it gets four times stronger when the distance halves.

Because the forces are equal but the masses differ, the lighter body accelerates more (a = F/m). This is why it swings around more widely.

The centre of mass

The white crosshair marks the centre of mass. No outside force acts on the pair, so this point moves in a straight line at constant speed, or stays still. Both bodies orbit it, not each other's centre.

A two-body problem therefore reduces to a one-body problem: a single "reduced mass" μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂) orbiting a fixed centre.

Orbit shapes

Total energy decides the shape. E < 0: bound (circle or ellipse). E = 0: parabola, just escaping. E > 0: hyperbola, the bodies fly apart for good.

The gravity-well grid is a picture of the potential energy, with deeper dips near heavier masses. It is a visual analogy only and plays no part in the computation.

The numerics

Each frame runs many Velocity Verlet steps:

x ← x + vΔt + ½aΔt²
a′ = F(x)/m
v ← v + ½(a + a′)Δt

Verlet is symplectic: the energy error oscillates but does not grow. You can check this in the Energy error chart. If you make Δt larger, the wobble grows as Δt².
